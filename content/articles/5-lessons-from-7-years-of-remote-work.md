I have worked full time remotely since 2017, on projects and at companies of nearly every size and niche. The pandemic certainly accelerated the shift, especially in software development. Remote work today is not a trend, it is a reality that changes how we interact with our teams and how we get things done. But for it to work well, you need practices that support inclusion, productivity and wellbeing.

Here are some lessons from the last few years.

**1. If everyone is remote, everyone is part of the team**

Working remotely means everyone should have the same opportunity to participate and engage, wherever they are. Nobody should feel isolated or pushed into a secondary role. Encourage active collaboration in discussions and decisions, and treat the flexibility of remote work as the standard rather than as a perk.

**2. Build on trust**

Autonomy is essential in remote work. Micromanagement is not productive and it undermines the team's motivation. Set clear goals and shared objectives, and let each person own their contribution. That strengthens both collective responsibility and individual leadership.

**3. Avoid the always-on trap**

Flexibility is one of the advantages of remote work, but do not become its hostage. Working without clear boundaries drains your energy and your focus. Build an organised routine with daily, weekly and monthly goals, and set limits so you actually have time to rest and recharge.

**4. Remote work is collaborative**

Working remotely does not mean isolation. Regular communication between team members is fundamental. Small gestures — greeting your colleagues at the start of the day, asking about what they are into — strengthen the bonds and make the team feel closer.

**5. Create rituals to replace the office**

When the commute no longer marks the start of your day, you need new habits. Build a morning routine that switches your brain into work mode, whether that is tidying your space or some other ritual that works for you. For me, working in pyjamas is impossible. I need to be at least minimally put together to start the day.

**Bonus: remote work is not the same as working from home**

Remote work goes beyond being at home. It is the freedom to work from anywhere. It redefines how teams collaborate and connect, prioritising autonomy, flexibility and results. At home, in a café or in another country, what matters is finding an environment that works for you.

Working remotely is a process of learning and adapting. Over time, you and your team can develop practices that are more efficient and better aligned with what everyone needs.

Have you worked remotely? Share your experiences so we can keep learning together.

**Happy remote work.**
