Working remotely sounds like freedom. And it is, once you figure out the structure that keeps you sane.

## Routine is freedom

The biggest trap is thinking remote means no schedule. Without a routine, every hour feels like it could be a work hour. That's not freedom, it's anxiety.

I wake up at the same time regardless of timezone. Morning is for deep work: code, writing, architecture decisions. Afternoons are for calls, reviews, and admin. Evenings are off.

## Tools that matter

- **Terminal + Neovim**: my actual office
- **Linear**: async project tracking that doesn't require a meeting
- **Notion**: long-form docs, specs, decision logs
- **Slack**: with aggressive notification rules

The tool matters less than the discipline. Pick one, commit, move on.

## The loneliness problem

Co-working spaces help. So does having a small group of people you talk to every week, not about work, just about life. Remote doesn't have to mean isolated.

## The real advantage

You get to design your environment. The right music, the right light, the right chair. No open-plan office noise. No commute eating two hours of your day.

That's the actual superpower: control over your context.
